Figure 5. Simplified construction of fig. 22 in the third edition of Mariotte’s Traitté de la percussion (1684). The construction considers balls without rolling, assumes perfect ressort, and abstracts from impediments to motion. Segments representing motion correspond to distances traversed in equal times and are therefore proportional to speeds. The bodies’ weights are in the ratio A∶B = 1∶3.

0°: direct impact; 90°: grazing limit. Move right for more grazing contact.

A’s weight is taken as 1 and its incoming speed as 100. Values are rounded; totals and the surplus are calculated before rounding.

Each body’s quantity of motion is the product of weight and speed. The calculator applies Mariotte’s rules in II, prop. III, 213–17. The quantities are added without regard to direction, as in his direct-impact example of a lighter body striking a heavier one (I, prop. XXVI, consec. II, 174–75).

Reading the Figure

  1. The lighter body A moves along Aa and strikes obliquely the heavier body B, initially at rest. At impact, their centres occupy the points labelled a and B; the line of centres is extended in both directions to D and F.
  2. A’s incoming motion resolves into the lateral component AD, parallel to the common tangent at contact, and the perpendicular component Da, along the line of centres, forming the right-angled triangle ADa.
  3. Were the bodies equal, B would move to F (BF = Da), while A would move to H (aH = AD).
  4. With B three times as heavy as A, Mariotte’s rules for direct impact give B half the perpendicular speed, so it moves to I (BI = IF = Da/2).
  5. A retains its lateral component (aH = AD), while its perpendicular component is reversed and halved (aC = Da/2). Combining aH with HL, drawn parallel and equal to aC, gives aL, which represents A’s outgoing speed and direction. As the hypotenuse of the right-angled triangle aHL, aL exceeds aH = AD.
  6. For this weight ratio, the total quantity of motion, reckoned without regard to direction, is proportional to Aa before impact and to aL + 3BI afterwards. Both terms of the outgoing sum exceed the corresponding sides of the incoming triangle ADa: aL exceeds AD, and 3BI = 3Da/2 exceeds Da. Their sum therefore exceeds AD + Da, which itself exceeds the hypotenuse Aa. B therefore gains more quantity of motion than A loses.
  7. Increasingly grazing contact brings the incoming path closer to a right angle with the line of centres (slide right). Da and HL then tend to zero, while AD = aH approaches the fixed incoming length Aa. A thus retains almost all its speed, and its deflection tends to zero. The surplus remains positive for every oblique impact but tends to zero in the grazing limit.

  2. A’s incoming motion resolves into the lateral component AD, parallel to the common tangent at contact, and the perpendicular component Da, along the line of centres, forming the right-angled triangle ADa.
  3. The bodies being equal, B takes the whole of Da and moves to F (BF = Da).
  4. A, keeping AD alone, moves to H (aH = AD), so that the two depart at right angles.
  5. With equal weights, the total quantity of motion, reckoned without regard to direction, is proportional to Aa before impact and to aH + BF = AD + Da afterwards. These two sides of the right-angled triangle ADa together exceed its hypotenuse Aa, so the total increases. B therefore gains more quantity of motion than A loses.
