Reading the Figure

  1. A moves along Aa and strikes B, which is at rest. At the moment of impact their centres are at a and B, and the line of centres is DaF.
  2. A’s motion resolves into the lateral AD and the perpendicular Da.
  3. With B three times as heavy as A, Mariotte’s rules for direct impact give B only half the perpendicular speed, so it moves to I (BI = IF = Da/2).
  4. A retains its lateral component (aH = AD), while its perpendicular component is reversed and halved (aC = Da/2). Combining aH with HL, drawn parallel and equal to aC, gives aL, A’s outgoing speed and direction.
  5. Taken without regard to direction, the total quantity of motion increases: after impact it is proportional to aL + 3BI, before it to Aa.
  6. With Aa held fixed, a more grazing encounter (slider to the right) leaves A almost all its speed and changes its direction only slightly, while B still gains more quantity of motion than A loses.
  1. A moves along Aa and strikes B, which is at rest. At the moment of impact their centres are at a and B, and the line of centres is DaBF.
  2. A’s motion resolves into the lateral AD and the perpendicular Da.
  3. The bodies being equal, B takes the whole of Da and moves to F (BF = Da).
  4. A, keeping AD alone, moves to H (aH = AD), so that the two depart at right angles.
  5. Taken without regard to direction, the total quantity of motion increases: after impact it is proportional to aH + BF, before it to Aa.
  6. With Aa held fixed, a more grazing encounter (slider to the right) leaves A almost all its speed and changes its direction only slightly, while B still gains more quantity of motion than A loses.

Lengths are proportional to speeds. Each quantity of motion is the product of weight and speed, with A’s weight taken as 1. Mariotte gives no reckoning of the quantity of motion in this proposition (II, prop. III, 213–17); the sums are taken here without regard to direction, as in his numerical examples for direct impact (I, prop. XXVI, consec. II, 174–75).
